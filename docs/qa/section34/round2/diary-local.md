# Playtest diary: the Panjim local (round 2)

## 1. Who I am and what I wanted

I've lived in Panjim all my life. I walk the Campal promenade, I know which Fontainhas lanes go under water every monsoon, and I've watched the Taleigao and Merces fields fill up with houses. Nobody told me anything about the game. I wanted to play one full run of "Play Panjim" to its last card, and to check every name, label and piece of geography against the city I know. I zoomed and panned around the board as I played.

Honest caveat up front: I had to play the opening three times. The game sent me back to the main menu twice in the middle of a run and wiped my progress (see section 7). The results below are from my third run, which went all the way to the final card.

## 2. The play, in order

**Run 1 (lost at 2030)**
- **Menu, intro.** "Twenty five years of Panjim. Three storms. One build a year." Clear. The intro map shows a cyclone path aimed at Sharada Mandir School in Miramar. That made sense to me straight away.
- **2026-2029.** Maya's card asked for 1 dune and 2 pandanus in front of the school. I clicked the highlighted sand hexes: dune (homes at risk 9 to 4), pandanus (4 to 3), pandanus (3 to 0). My **first build came at about 75 s**, after about 6 actions. It felt fine, but very much "do what the card says".
- **2029.** A "Get ahead" objective asked for a khazan in Merces. I panned east, found the highlighted hex and built it. Nice to see khazans in a game at all.
- **2030, before the 5th build.** I read the How to play page, toggled R, and tried a creek tile (only a small dam is allowed there, and it "doesn't change the next storm"). I pressed Escape and turned the mouse wheel, and found myself on the **main menu**. The run was gone. Play Panjim started again at 2026.

**Run 2 (lost at 2035)**
- Same opening. Sharada Mandir and pandanus again, then the khazan in Merces. For the 5th build I put a mangrove next to that khazan, to get ahead on the flood.
- **2030 cyclone.** It played for about 6 seconds. ★★★, 9 of 9 saved. "Miramar Beach: Damaged", "Sharada Mandir School: Stood", "Dhempe College: Stood". What protected the homes: dune 5, pandanus 4. Very clear, but nothing was ever at stake.
- **Flood brief.** "Monsoon rain swells the Mandovi upstream; the flood comes down it through the channel at Ribandar into Merces, St Cruz and Taleigao." 49 homes at risk. The checklist: 2 mangroves and 2 khazans in Merces, 3 khazans in St Cruz, 1 mangrove in Taleigao.
- **2031-2035.** Mangrove, mangrove (49 to 44 to 43), khazan, khazan (43 to 38). Then I clicked the first St Cruz tile and was **back on the main menu again**. I had lost ten years of building.

**Run 3 (the full run)**
- A third reset dropped me on the menu, and my next click on the map landed on the Tutorial button. I had to go Menu, "Leave the Tutorial", Leave, then Play Panjim again. After that I moved the mouse between every step, out of fear.
- **2026-2030.** Same as before: dune, pandanus, pandanus, khazan in Merces, mangrove in Merces. **Cyclone 2030: ★★★, 9/9.** This time a replay played first: "With no defences at all: 9 homes hit." That was good.
- **2031-2035.** 2 mangroves in Merces (49 to 44 to 43). I toggled R and the risk map made me sit up. **Patto and the Fontainhas edge along the Ourem creek were red**, and so was a big block of Taleigao. That is exactly what floods every July. Then 2 khazans at the top of the Merces channel (43 to 38).
- **2036-2038.** 2 khazans in St Cruz (38 to 35 to 31). Mangrove on the Taleigao side (31 to **20**, the best single build of the game). Third St Cruz khazan (20 to 16).
- **2039.** Maya said Taleigao was most at risk. I picked my own tile, a wetland hex next to the red block south of Altinho, and planted a mangrove (16 to 13). It counted for "Plant 1 mangrove at Taleigao".
- **2040, last build.** "Last call! 13 homes ... around the city centre." The Ourem creek tile between Fontainhas and Patto only takes a small dam. I built it (biodiversity −1). **Flood 2040: ★★, 48 of 57 saved.** "9 homes were lost; three stars allow 8 at most." I missed three stars by one home. The breakdown: khazan 28, mangrove 14, small dam 4. The dam was a good call. "Goa State Museum: Stood." I smiled.
- **2050 brief.** "Cyclone and flood ... the waves and the river come together." The places named were Our Lady of the Immaculate Conception, Adil Shah Palace and Mahalaxmi Temple. 30 homes at risk. The checklist: 1 mangrove at the Ourem creek, and "8 khazans at 5 places, shown on the map".
- **2041-2043.** I clicked the highlighted tiles to read their cards, then clicked lower down the screen to close each card. That click landed on the card's Mangrove button, so I **planted 3 mangroves on the Ourem creek by accident** (30 to 25). I lost three years without meaning to.
- **2044-2045.** The cyclone side. Beach tiles near Campal said "This doesn't change the next storm". I probed sea tiles one by one and found two where a breakwater helps. Breakwater (25 to 19), then a second breakwater (19 to 17).
- **2046-2047.** 2 khazans by Patto and the Goa State Museum (17 to 16 to 15).
- **2048.** "Campal is most at risk." I built a dune on the one beach hex in front of Campal that the card said would help: 15 to **15**, no change.
- **2049.** Seawall on the sea in front of Campal (−5 biodiversity): 15 to 14.
- **2050, last build.** Breakwater (−2 biodiversity, which took me to +38). **Storm 2050: ★★, 43 of 56 saved.** The whole Campal strip, from Kala Academy to Campal Garden, went dark. "The wind alone took 4 of the homes lost. In this game pandanus shields the homes within 2 tiles of it." I wish I'd been told that before the storm. Institute Menezes Braganza, Don Bosco High School, Kala Academy and Campal Garden all "Damaged".
- **Final card.** "Panjim, 2026 to 2050": 100 of 122 homes saved, **7 of 9 stars, Panjim cleared**, +38 biodiversity, "Short of the +40 three stars need".

## 3. Places, names and geography

**What felt right**

- **Miramar beach runs as one strip of sand down to Caranzalem and Dona Paula**, with the cyclone coming in off the Arabian Sea from the west. Sharada Mandir School and Dhempe College are in Miramar. (local-miramar-start.jpg, left middle)
- **Altinho is a raised plateau**, taller hexes in the middle of the city, with the **Government Polytechnic on top**. That is correct, and it's the high ground I'd run to. (local-fontainhas-altinho.jpg, centre)
- **Kala Academy and Campal Garden are on the Campal riverfront.** Adil Shah Palace faces the river, the Church of the Immaculate Conception is in the centre, and Mahalaxmi Temple sits just below it at the foot of Altinho. (local-city-labels-2041.jpg, top)
- **Patto is across the Ourem creek from Fontainhas**, with the Goa State Museum on the Patto side and the Atal Setu label out over the Mandovi beside it. São Tomé is next to Fontainhas. All correct. (local-city-labels-2041.jpg, top right)
- **The monsoon risk map**: Patto and the Fontainhas edge along the Ourem creek, plus low Taleigao, all red. Every Panjimkar knows those streets go under water. This was the moment the game felt like my city. (local-flood-risk-2033.jpg)
- **Khazans in Merces and St Cruz**, "Farmers and fishers share it", and Maya's Lobo (2020) facts about bunds and sluice gates breaching. These are real Goa things, and they're the best text in the game.
- North of the Mandovi (Betim, Reis Magos) is shown but marked "North of the Mandovi: not part of this game". Fair enough.
- A seawall along the Campal front feels real; the promenade wall is there.

**What felt wrong**

- **"The Mandovi waterfront" label is in the wrong place.** On screen it sits far inland to the east, by the Merces/St Cruz channel (local-city-labels-2041.jpg, about x 1263, y 555). To any local, the Mandovi waterfront is D.B. Marg, the riverfront from Campal past Adil Shah Palace to the jetty, along the north edge of the city. That label would make Goans laugh.
- **The creeks are drawn as rivers.** The north-south water between Taleigao/Altinho and Merces/St Cruz is 2 to 4 hexes wide and looks like a second Mandovi (local-merces-channel.jpg, left half). The St Inez creek is also a broad blue band (local-fontainhas-altinho.jpg, centre left). In real life the Ourem and St Inez creeks are narrow nullahs with khazan marsh around them. I accept a simplified board, but these look like major rivers.
- **Ribandar is never labelled**, even though Maya's flood text says the flood comes "through the channel at Ribandar". I had to guess it was the top-right water. Caranzalem gets no game label either (only faint map text), while Dona Paula does.
- **The spellings are mixed.** "Santa Inez" is Portuguese but "St Cruz" is English, side by side. Locals say "St Inez" and "Santa Cruz" or "St Cruz" loosely, but the mix on one map looks unedited.
- **Jama Masjid is too far west.** It's placed out by Don Bosco College and Kala Academy, towards Campal (local-city-labels-2041.jpg, about x 693, y 303). I'd put it in the centre, close to Mahalaxmi Temple. I'm less sure about the Municipal Market, which also felt pushed north-west, next to Institute Menezes Braganza.
- **The area names in objectives are fuzzy.** One "St Cruz" khazan tile is on the Taleigao side of the channel. In 2039 the "mangrove at Taleigao" objective tag showed on hexes near Merces, while the tile that actually counted was south of Altinho, which I'd call Mala or St Inez.
- **The "Miramar Beach" label sits on a block of houses**, not on the sand (early Miramar screenshots).
- **The trees are the wrong kind.** Pandanus is drawn as coconut-palm clumps. On Miramar the beach trees a local pictures are casuarinas (suru). Pandanus (kevda) on Miramar is not the image in my head.
- **Khazans are drawn as small round ponds with a ring bund.** A khazan is a wide flat paddy field with bunds and a sluice gate (mano). The circle looks like a fish tank.
- **The first fact is from England and Wales.** It's about sand dunes, and it's the first fact you see. It sounds foreign right after a Goa intro. The Goa facts land much better.

## 4. What confused me

- **The red-and-cream striped double line across the map**, from the Mandovi down past Taleigao and around the bay. Hovering explains nothing. Is it the city limit? A road? A one-line legend or a tooltip would have helped. (Visible in most screenshots.)
- **Which builds actually help with wind?** Near Campal, one beach hex "changes the next storm", but a dune there saved 0 homes. A seawall at −5 biodiversity saved 1. The answer, pandanus within 2 tiles, only appeared on the result card afterwards. Before the storm, a risk view that split wind from water, or a card line like "shields homes within 2 tiles", would have helped.
- **I had to probe tile by tile.** The useful sea tiles were not highlighted, so I clicked a dozen hexes one by one to read "This doesn't change the next storm". Highlighting every useful tile, not just the objective ones, would save that.
- **Earlier builds don't count.** My Merces khazan from before the cyclone did not count toward "Build 2 khazans in Merces" (0/2). Either count it, or say "new khazans".
- **"Build 8 khazans at 5 places, shown on the map" with 10 builds**, while the cyclone side also needed builds. I couldn't tell whether the 8 were really needed, so I ignored the list.
- **Closing a build card.** Clicking outside the card to close it sometimes lands on the card and builds, because the card moves around (once it opened far from its tile). An "×" or close button, or a confirm step, would help.
- **Escape** closes a card if one is open, otherwise it opens "Leave Panjim?". That one is fine, but it scared me after the resets.

## 7. Bugs and glitches

1. **MAJOR (a blocker for a slow, reading player): the game drops to the main menu mid-run and wipes all progress.**
   - Steps: Play Panjim, make several builds, then spend a few minutes reading the How to play page or looking at screenshots, without clicking (or pressing only keys and the wheel).
   - It happened twice: once at 2030 (year 5, after 4 builds), and once at 2035 (year 10, after 9 builds).
   - Expected: a visible "Still there?" warning, and if the run ends, a Continue option.
   - Got: straight to the menu. I never caught a warning on screen. Play Panjim restarts at 2026, with no resume. (local-kicked-to-menu.jpg)
   - If this is the 3-minute idle rule, it is far too harsh for a 25-year run, and a player who steps away for chai loses 20 minutes of play.
2. **MINOR: after a reset, a click on the map starts the Tutorial.** The menu appears under the cursor, so my next "map" click landed on the Tutorial button and opened "How this works / Tutorial". I needed Menu, Leave the Tutorial, then Leave.
3. **MINOR: the 2040 result card reads three stars in its text but shows two.** The visible stars are two, but the card's text reads "Most of your city held. ★★★", while the top bar correctly shows ★★☆. A screen reader would announce three stars. (local-flood-card-2040.jpg)
4. **MINOR: the homes-lost count doesn't match between cards.** The 2050 card says "Lost in earlier storms: 7", but the 2040 card said 9 were lost, and the final summary says 22 lost in all (9 + 13). (local-final-storm-card.jpg, local-end-summary.jpg)
5. **MINOR: there's no undo after an accidental build.** In 2041-2043, clicking to close the card built Mangrove three times. Expected: a click outside the card does nothing to the card itself, or a build asks for confirmation, or there's an undo.
6. **MINOR: a build card opened far from its tile.** In 2035, the card for a Merces tile at about (1105, 385) opened down at about (1297, 771).
7. **MINOR: the Campal dune had no effect.** In 2048 the card said this dune would change the next storm. Homes at risk stayed at 15 after building it.
8. **MINOR (copy):** "Got it" on Maya's very first bubble couldn't be clicked twice early on. The bubble seemed to vanish by itself.
9. **Console:** no errors or warnings at any check, including the final /console (`{"messages": []}`).

## 11. Verdict

A Panjim a local recognises (Altinho, Patto, Campal, the khazans) with a satisfying flood puzzle, let down by a trivial first cyclone, an opaque last storm at Campal, a misplaced "Mandovi waterfront" label, and resets that wiped my run twice.

## Knowledge questions (asked after play)

All five answers come only from what the game showed me.

1. **What does a mangrove do in a storm?**
   It slows the water before it reaches the houses, in a cyclone or a flood. Its card says "Slows cyclone surge and waves, and river floods too", and Maya said 100 m of mangroves cut wave height by 13% to 66%, mostly for waves under 70 cm.

2. **Why can a seawall be risky?**
   It "costs nature, and cracks with age" (−5 biodiversity on the card). Maya said it holds back a cyclone's surge only until it breaks: in its second cyclone, or in its next one once it is 10 years old.

3. **What is a khazan, and what is it for?**
   It holds back river floods: it keeps floodwater in its fields, so nearby homes stay drier, and "farmers and fishers share it". Maya's Lobo (2020) facts showed it has bunds and sluice gates that breach if they're not kept up, and that mangroves grow back over the old fields when a bund breaks.

4. **Where did the 2040 flood come from, and why from there rather than the sea?**
   The brief said monsoon rain swells the Mandovi upstream, and the flood comes down the river through the channel at Ribandar into Merces, St Cruz and Taleigao. It comes from the river because it's monsoon rain, not a sea storm; the game didn't explain any more than that.

5. **What helps homes against a cyclone's wind?**
   Pandanus. Its card says "Shields homes from cyclone waves and wind", and the 2050 result card said "The wind alone took 4 of the homes lost. In this game pandanus shields the homes within 2 tiles of it." Maya also said dunes and pandanus shield the homes behind the beach from a cyclone, but only pandanus was tied to wind.
