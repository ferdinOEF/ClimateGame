# Playtest diary: the coastal scientist (Section 34, round 2)

## 1. Who I am and what I wanted

I work in coastal geomorphology and nature-based defences: mangrove belts, dune systems, strand vegetation such as pandanus, and the hard options (seawalls, breakwaters). I know Goa's khazans and the Mandovi estuary. I wanted two things. First, a fair game about Panjim that a non-specialist could finish. Second, and more important to me, a game that never puts a game mechanic in a lab coat. My standard: a claim about the real world must be true, must name its source and must say no more than that source says. Anything that is only how the game works must be labelled as a game rule.

I read Facts & sources first, then played the whole Tutorial, then one full run of Play Panjim to its final card. Afterwards I opened six of the cited sources on the web.

What I found in the sources I opened:
- Down To Earth 2020 (bandharas) does not say bandharas protect against floods. It calls them irrigation check dams, closed in Sept–Oct and opened in May. It warns that mud bandharas are risky in heavy rain and can cause flash floods. The game's small-dam rule says the opposite.
- Lobo 2020 says bunds breach through lack of maintenance and that mangroves then reclaim the fields. It says nothing about flood protection. The Field Guide's "never needs a repair" contradicts it.
- McIvor 2012 Report 2: surge reductions of about 5–50 cm per km of mangrove width, biggest at the seaward edge. Nothing on river floods.
- PROTA: "soil stabilization", "coastal windbreaks", mats in Asia. The game's facts are faithful. It also says pandanus plants are harvested from 2.5 to 3 years and flower at 3 to 4 years: they are not mature in the year they are planted.
- Sahapedia: the outer bund mangroves are "wave breakers against tidal action". Sluice gates "flush out the water" during the monsoon. It gives no support for "khazans hold floodwater".

## 4. Game rules that might teach something false about the real world

1. **Nature-based defences work in full from the day they are planted** (pandanus, mangrove). This is the biggest one. Real mangrove planting takes 5 to 15 years to form a protective belt, and many plantings fail. Pandanus takes years to establish (PROTA: 2.5 to 4 years to harvest or flowering). Because a seawall is also "strong from the day it's built", the game removes the main real trade-off: fast grey versus slow green. A player leaves thinking a mangrove planted the year before a cyclone saves a street.
2. **"A khazan is never worn, never needs a repair."** It contradicts the game's own cited Lobo fact that bunds breach when not maintained. The lesson Goa most needs from khazans is that they depend on community upkeep.
3. **Small dam "holds back river floods".** The game's only source on bandharas says they are water storage, opened in floods, and dangerous when mud ones fail. It teaches the reverse of the source.
4. **Mangroves as a strong river-flood defence** (11 homes in the tutorial flood, 21 in 2040). Nothing cited supports a fluvial-flood benefit. In an estuarine channel, dense vegetation can even raise water levels upstream. The cited benefit is waves and surge.
5. **Scale.** One 135 m mangrove tile takes 11 homes out of harm. McIvor's figure (5–50 cm of surge per km) means a 135 m belt changes surge by centimetres. One breakwater tile removing 12 of 22 homes is similarly generous. The game is an abstraction, but players read the numbers as the size of the effect.
6. **Seawalls and breakwaters break at 10 years old, or in their second cyclone.** Real structures are designed for decades, with maintenance. The honest lesson is "costly upkeep, and they can fail or be overtopped". As written it teaches "hard defences are disposable".
7. **"Pandanus is the only shelter from wind."** Mangrove forests and coastal tree belts (e.g. casuarina) are also windbreaks. This is fine while every mention carries "in this game", but the tutorial summary card does not make that clear.
8. **Seawall built in open water** (tutorial). That is not a seawall by the game's own definition.

## 7. Where I smiled

- The Facts & sources screen. Scoped claims ("in England and Wales", "waves under 70 cm"), blogs flagged "As reported by", missing dates flagged, DEM limits explained, the height exaggeration labelled. I rarely see this, even in grown-up outreach.
- The 2040 replay "With no defences at all: 57 homes hit". It is a counterfactual made visible, and the best teaching moment in the game.
- "Defences cover for each other, so the rows need not add up". Honest attribution.
- Seawalls push water to their ends: real flanking, nicely shown.
- The 2050 "cyclone and flood" with "Where they meet" hatching teaches compound flooding, a real and under-taught idea.
- Real landmarks in the right places (Sharada Mandir, Dhempe College, Kala Academy, Goa State Museum at Patto). Khazans and mangroves side by side is a proper Goan lesson.

## 11. Would I play again? Would I tell a colleague?

**Play again: yes, once.** I'd play to try to save Campal in 2050 with pandanus placed for the wind, and to see whether ignoring the objectives does better. Beyond that, the middle 15 builds are too guided to replay for fun.

**Tell a colleague: yes, with a caveat.** I'd say: "The sourcing is the most careful I've seen in an outreach game, and it gets Goa's khazans and mangroves right. But in the game, mangroves and pandanus work at full strength the day you plant them, khazans never need upkeep, and small dams hold back floods. Use it to start the conversation, not as the lesson."

## 12. Verdict in one line

Superbly sourced and honestly labelled facts. But a few labelled game rules (instant mangroves and pandanus, khazans that never need upkeep, flood-stopping bandharas) contradict the game's own sources, and those are the parts players will remember.

## Knowledge questions (asked after play)

Answered only from what the game showed me.

1. **What does a mangrove do in a storm?** It slows the flow of water, which lowers a cyclone's surge and cuts the height of its waves (the cited studies: 13–66% lower wave height over 100 m). In the game it also slows a river flood and shelters the streets behind it.
2. **Why can a seawall be risky?** In the game it pushes water round its ends onto the land there, breaks in its second cyclone (or its next one once it is 10 years old), and costs the most biodiversity (5) because nothing lives on it.
3. **What is a khazan, and what is it for?** A khazan is a Goan coastal wetland reclaimed from marsh. A bund and a sluice gate surround paddy fields and fish ponds, the bunds keep the tide out, and farmers and fishers share the land. In the game it holds back river floods (it holds the floodwater so homes nearby stay drier) and adds biodiversity.
4. **Where did the 2040 flood come from, and why from there rather than the sea?** It was a monsoon flood: in the game, monsoon rain swells the Mandovi upstream, and the flood comes down through the channel at Ribandar into Merces, St Cruz and Taleigao. The game only says it is rain swelling the river; it did not explain any more why it doesn't come from the sea, so beyond that, I don't know.
5. **What helps homes against a cyclone's wind?** Pandanus. In the game it is the only defence that lowers the wind: homes within 2 tiles of it take half the wind damage. (The real-world fact shown was that pandanus is planted as coastal windbreaks.)
