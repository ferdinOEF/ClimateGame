# Playtest diary: the casual, story-and-city player (round 2)

## 1. Who I am and what I wanted

I play for the place and the people. I'm not in a hurry, I read what the characters say, I zoom in on neighbourhoods and hover over landmarks, and I want to see the city change over time. I care more about the homes than the score. Plan: do the Tutorial, then one full run of Play Panjim to its final card.

## 2. The play, in order

Times are what I think a real player would spend (about 3 s a click, 4 words a second), not my own thinking time.

**Menu (about 8 s).** A pretty board behind a clean card: "Twenty five years of Panjim. Three storms. One build a year." I knew right away what kind of game this was. I picked Tutorial.

**Tutorial, 2026 to 2032 (about 4.5 min).**
- The intro card has a clear little cone map ("The beach", "The estuary"). Maya, the guide, appears and says the glowing card and tiles show what to try.
- 2026: I clicked a glowing beach tile and a card menu came up (Dune, Pandanus, Seawall, plus "Not here: ..."). I built a Dune. **First build at about 60 s, my 5th action.** Homes at risk went from 22 to 18, which felt good.
- 2027 Pandanus, 2028 Breakwater on the glowing water, 2029 Seawall. Homes at risk dropped to 2. Then the cyclone came. It lasted about 6 seconds: some rain and a small swirl. 3 stars, 22/22 saved. The card shows each defence's share (Dune 4, Pandanus 6, Breakwater 10, Seawall 2), which was nice.
- 2030 Mangrove, 2031 Khazan, 2032 Small dam. The river flood was over in about 4 seconds: 3 stars, 26/26. The card said the cyclone defences did 0 against the flood ("A cyclone defence; this was a river flood"). That was clear and useful.
- The end card, "You tried 7 of 7 defences", has a one-line summary of each defence. That's a good cheat sheet.
- Feeling: smooth and pleasant, but it's on rails. I never had to think.

**Play Panjim intro, and a nasty surprise.** I clicked "Play Panjim" on the tutorial's end card. The Panjim intro appeared (about 75 words; I read it). When I went to click Begin, I was **back on the main menu**. I didn't touch anything and didn't see a "Still there?" warning. I clicked Play Panjim again from the menu and it worked.

**2026 to 2030, the first cyclone (about 5.5 min of Panjim time, a lot of it spent exploring).**
- Maya: "Hello! I am Maya ... protect Sharada Mandir School." There's an objective card at the bottom right.
- I did what I came for and wandered off. I zoomed into Fontainhas. It's lovely: little boats on the Mandovi, people on the streets, palm trees. Hovering over buildings gives names: **Mahalaxmi Temple**, **Jama Masjid**, **Institute Menezes Braganza (Library and gallery)**, **Adil Shah Palace (Old Secretariat)**. This was the best part of the game for me. Hovering over an ordinary house tile gives nothing; clicking it says only "House".
- Pressing Escape to close something opened "Leave Panjim? You haven't built anything yet." I clicked Stay. Zooming out with the wheel did nothing at first, while the "House" tag was open.
- 2026 Dune in front of the school (at risk 9 to 4). 2027 Pandanus (to 3), 2028 Pandanus (to 0). Objective done. A new one: "Get ahead: the monsoon flood: Build 1 khazan in Merces." I had to pan around to find Merces, but the label is there. 2029 Khazan in Merces.
- 2030, last build before the cyclone, with 0 homes at risk. Every tile I tried said "This doesn't change the next storm", so I planted a pandanus for nature. Along the way I read How to play (?). It's long, but it finally told me the rule for stars.
- **Cyclone 2030: 3 stars, 9 of 9 saved.** Miramar Beach "Damaged", the school and Dhempe College "Stood". The storm itself was a few seconds of light rain. A bit flat after five years of build-up.

**2031 to 2040, the monsoon flood.**
- A "Coming up" card with a cone map and Maya: the Mandovi swells and comes down through Ribandar into Merces, St Cruz and Taleigao. I liked this. It gave the flood a story.
- Homes at risk: 50. The objectives were 1 mangrove in Taleigao, 3 khazans in St Cruz, 2 mangroves and 2 khazans in Merces. That's 8 of my 10 builds already decided.
- I followed the glowing tiles. Each build knocked 3 to 11 homes off the count (50, 45, 42, 39, 36, 33, 29, 18, 14). Maya dropped real facts with sources ("100 m of mangroves cut wave height by 13% to 66%", Lobo 2020 on khazan bunds). A **white egret flew over Merces**. That made me smile.
- 2039: "Objective done." The new get-ahead was a mangrove near Atal Setu, but Maya said "Taleigao is most at risk: strengthen it now." I clicked six tiles around the red homes in Taleigao and Altinho: "House", "Nothing can be built on this tile", "This doesn't change the next storm". I gave up and planted the Atal Setu mangrove (at risk 14 to 10).
- 2040: "Last call! 10 homes are still in danger, most of them around Taleigao." I found one wetland tile next to the red cluster south of Altinho with no "doesn't change" warning, and built a khazan there.
- **Flood 2040: 3 stars, 51 of 57 saved, 6 lost.** There was a replay ("6 homes hit, 51 homes wet but standing"). The lost homes were in **Fontainhas, Patto and Merces, not Taleigao**, which is where Maya had told me to look. Goa State Museum "Damaged", Campal Garden "Stood". "What protected": Khazan 31, Mangrove 9.

**2041 to 2050, the cyclone and flood together.**
- The "Coming up" card shows both cones and "where they meet". Then the map opened with a dozen landmark labels at once: Our Lady of the Immaculate Conception, Panjim Municipal Market, Don Bosco High School and College, Kala Academy, Government Polytechnic and more. Panjim really looked like Panjim here.
- Objectives: 2 mangroves and **7 khazans** at "5 places, shown on the map". That's 9 of my 10 builds. But the cyclone arrow pointed straight at Campal's beach.
- 2041: I used my one spare build on a pandanus at Campal, where the arrow lands. **Homes at risk stayed at 28.** I couldn't tell whether it had done anything. (The final card says it saved 3.)
- 2042 to 2048: a mangrove, then khazans on the glowing tiles. A blue kingfisher-like bird turned up. At risk went 25, 24, 22, 21, 16.
- 2049: Maya: "Campal is most at risk: strengthen it now." I built a Dune on the Campal beach tile right beside the red homes. It gave no "doesn't change" warning. **Homes at risk stayed at 15.** The other sand and sea tiles nearby were "Nothing can be built" or "doesn't change". The red homes sit on the waterfront with no beach in front of them.
- 2050, last build: the remaining mangrove objective at the Mandovi waterfront. Then the storm.
- **The 2050 storm was the only dramatic one**: dark blue water poured over the Campal coast and Fontainhas, with rain. Afterwards the whole Campal waterfront row was blackened. 13 lost. "The water has gone down and the sun is out again." I liked that line.
- **2050: 2 stars, 44 of 57 saved.** The Municipal Market, Menezes Braganza, Don Bosco High School and College and 3 more were "Damaged". What protected: Khazan 33, Mangrove 26, Pandanus 3, **Dune 0**.
- **Final card: 104 of 123 homes saved, +60 biodiversity, 8 of 9 stars, "Panjim cleared".**

## 3. What confused me

1. **Maya points at a place where I can't build.** Twice ("Taleigao is most at risk", then "Campal is most at risk") she told me to strengthen an area, and I couldn't find a single tile there that would help. In 2040 the homes lost weren't even in Taleigao. *What would have helped:* when Maya names an area, make the tiles that would help it glow. If none exist, have her say so: "Campal's waterfront has no beach left to defend."
2. **The build menu never says how many homes a build saves.** It only says "This doesn't change the next storm" or nothing. My Campal dune had no warning, but it saved 0 and the count didn't move. *Helped:* a line on each card like "saves about N homes from the 2050 storm", or at least a warning that is reliable.
3. **Homes at risk didn't move after builds the result card later credited** (the 2041 pandanus: 28 stayed 28, but "saved 3"). I guess new homes were being added while my build removed some, but the game never shows the city growing. *Helped:* a short "+3 new homes built in Campal this year" note, or split the number.
4. **Objective place names don't match where you build.** "3 khazans in St Cruz": the glowing tiles were on the creek far from the St Cruz label, closer to Taleigao. *Helped:* draw the objective's area on the map, or put the label by the tiles.
5. **The objectives use up almost every build** (8 of 10, then 9 of 10). It felt like the game wanted me to follow orders. Was I allowed to ignore them? It never said what happens if I do. *Helped:* one line, "Objectives are suggestions; any build that lowers homes at risk counts."
6. **Landmark labels jump around** between years (Our Lady of the Immaculate Conception, Mahalaxmi Temple and Campal/Campal Garden swapped places and some disappeared). Once I'd learned where things were, they moved. *Helped:* fixed labels, or labels only on hover.
7. **Escape opens "Leave Panjim?"** when I only wanted to close a tag or menu. *Helped:* Escape closes the top thing first. It did close a build menu once, but with nothing open it went straight to Leave.
8. **"123 homes stood in its path"** on the whole-run card. Whose path? It covers three storms. Small wording issue.

## 4. Where I was bored

- **The first two storms are 4 to 6 seconds of light rain.** Five or ten years of build-up, then almost nothing to watch. I wanted the sea to rise against my dunes. Only the 2050 storm delivered.
- **2031 to 2048 was click the glow, pick the card tagged "Objective", repeat**, about 15 times. The facts from Maya kept it alive, but there was no decision in most of those years.
- **The 2030 last build**, with 0 at risk and every tile saying "doesn't change the next storm": a dead year.
- **The city doesn't visibly change over 25 years.** I came to watch Panjim grow and age. I didn't notice new houses, new buildings or anything moving with time, except my own defences and birds.

## 5. Where I smiled

- Hovering over Fontainhas and getting "Mahalaxmi Temple", "Jama Masjid", "Adil Shah Palace (Old Secretariat)". Little boats on the Mandovi, people on the streets.
- The white egret flying over Merces after I planted mangroves, and later the blue bird. Nature coming back, shown and not just counted.
- Maya's sourced facts (mangroves cutting wave height, khazan bunds breaching when nobody keeps them up). I learned something about Goa.
- The flood story: rain upstream, the Mandovi swelling, the flood coming through Ribandar.
- "The water has gone down and the sun is out again."
- The result cards that name real places: "Sharada Mandir School: Stood", "Goa State Museum: Damaged", "Lost in earlier storms: 6". It made the losses feel like they belonged to someone.
- The 2041 map with a dozen real landmark labels. That's Panjim.

## 9. Would I play again? Would I tell a friend?

**Play again: maybe once.** I'd do it to see whether I could save Campal in 2050 by building differently, and to hover over more landmarks. But I suspect the answer is "follow the glow, it's 8 stars anyway", and the middle 15 years are repetitive.

**Tell a friend: yes, a Goan friend or someone who loves Panjim.** I'd say: "It's a lovely little model of Panjim. You can hover over the Mahalaxmi Temple and the Old Secretariat, and you learn what khazans and mangroves actually do. Play the tutorial, then just follow the glowing tiles and enjoy the birds. Don't expect drama until the last storm." I wouldn't pitch it to a friend who wants a strategy game.

## 10. Verdict

A beautiful, well-sourced Panjim and a kind guide, but most years are "click the glow", and when Maya finally asks me to choose, the map doesn't let me act on what she says.

### The story and the city

Panjim looks alive in still pictures: the Fontainhas houses, the temple and mosque you can name by hovering, boats on the Mandovi, people walking, egrets over the khazans I planted. The 2041 view with a dozen real landmarks is the moment it felt like *my* city to protect. The khazan facts gave the place a past I didn't know. But it doesn't feel alive *over time*. Twenty-five years pass and I couldn't see Panjim grow, age or react, apart from my own defences. The people stay abstract: "homes at risk: 28", red stripes, a "House" tag. No family, no street name, nobody whose house it was. I did care, mostly in 2050, when the whole Campal waterfront row went black and the Municipal Market was "Damaged". That stung because I'd hovered over those names. Give me one named household on that row early on and I'd have cared a lot more.

## Knowledge questions (asked after play)

1. **What does a mangrove do in a storm?** Mangrove forests slow the flow of the water: they lower a storm's surge and cut the height of its waves, in a cyclone and in a river flood. Maya added a sourced fact that 100 m of mangroves cut wave height by 13% to 66%, mostly for waves under 70 cm.
2. **Why can a seawall be risky?** In this game it costs 5 biodiversity, the most of any defence. It pushes water to its ends, cracks with age, and breaks in its 2nd cyclone or once it is 10 years old.
3. **What is a khazan, and what is it for?** It is Goa's old farmed wetland, reclaimed from marsh: a bund and a sluice gate around paddy fields and fish ponds, shared by farmers and fishers, with a gate that lets water in and out. It holds back river floods (and adds biodiversity) but gives little help in a cyclone.
4. **Where did the 2040 flood come from, and why from there rather than the sea?** Monsoon rain swelled the Mandovi upstream, and the flood came down the river through the channel at Ribandar into Merces, St Cruz and Taleigao. The game's only reason was that the rain swells the river upstream, which makes it a river flood rather than a sea storm; beyond that I don't know.
5. **What helps homes against a cyclone's wind?** Pandanus (screw pine): a row of it shelters what is behind it from the wind. The game called it the only defence that shields homes from a cyclone's wind.
