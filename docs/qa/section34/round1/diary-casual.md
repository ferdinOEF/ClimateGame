# Playtest diary: casual player (story and city)

## 1. Who I am and what I wanted

I play for the story and the place. I like reading what characters say, zooming in on neighbourhoods and landmarks, and watching a city change over the years. I am not in a hurry and I don't min-max, but I care about the people in the homes. I wanted to do the Tutorial first, then one whole run of Play Panjim to its final card.

## 2. The play, in order

**Menu (about 10 s).** The menu is clean: "Twenty five years of Panjim. Three storms. One build a year." I knew right away what kind of game it was. I picked Tutorial.

**Tutorial, 2026 to 2029 (builds 1 to 4).** A "How this works" card with a little red heat map: the cyclone lands in 4 years. Maya (a ranger-type character with curly hair) explains that the glowing card and tiles show what to try. I clicked a glowing beach tile, and a menu of three cards came up (Dune, Pandanus, Seawall, each with a +/- nature badge). I built a Dune. **My first build came at about 65 s, after 4 actions.** Homes at risk went 22 → 18. Then Pandanus (→12), Breakwater out in the sea (→8), and Seawall (the storm starts as soon as you build the 4th). Easy and guided. It felt like a well-made toy.

**Tutorial cyclone, 2029.** Rain, waves, blue water rolling over the beach tiles I hadn't built on in the north. 1 home lost. Result: 3 stars, 21 of 22 saved, and a bar chart of what each defence saved (Dune 4, Pandanus 6, Breakwater 4, Seawall 7). Satisfying.

**Tutorial, 2030 to 2032.** Mangrove and Khazan on the estuary, Small dam on the river. Homes at risk went 25 → 19 → 11. The river flood saved 25 of 25, 3 stars. The card says the cyclone defences saved 0 "because this was a river flood", which taught me the two storm types clearly. The end card "You tried 7 of 7 defences" is a nice summary. The Tutorial took about 4.5 minutes.

**Panjim intro.** Now it's a real city: OpenStreetMap streets under the hexes, neighbourhood names (Miramar, Campal, Altinho, Fontainhas, Taleigao, Dona Paula). The intro text is longer (about 75 words) but readable. The first job is "Protect Sharada Mandir School": 1 dune and 2 pandanus.

**Looking around (2026).** Before building I zoomed into Altinho and Fontainhas. Little people walk around, a boat sits on the water, and hovering a landmark shows its name: Mahalaxmi Temple, Government Polytechnic, Kala Academy. I loved this part. Hovering an ordinary house tile shows nothing; clicking it says "House".

**2026 to 2028.** A dune in the arrow's path in front of the school (homes at risk 9 → 4), then pandanus north and south of it (→3 → 0). **First Panjim build at about 90 s into Panjim (about 13 actions, most of them me sightseeing).** Maya drops facts after each build ("2 of 20", "Game rule", sometimes "Source"). Then a new optional list: "Get ahead: the monsoon flood: build 1 khazan in Merces". I had to zoom out and pan to find Merces, where a glowing tile waited. I built it.

**2030, the last build before the cyclone.** Homes at risk was already 0. The build menu said "This doesn't change the next storm", which I appreciated. I planted a pandanus up the beach anyway, for later.

**Cyclone 2030.** Over in a few seconds: a pretty swirl in the sea, then "Your city came through", 3 stars, 9 of 9 saved. "Miramar Beach: Damaged, Sharada Mandir School: Stood, Dhempe College: Stood." It was a little anticlimactic; I'd prepared for a fight that never came.

**"Coming up: Monsoon flood in 2040, 10 years away."** Maya's game rule says the flood comes down the Mandovi, through the channel at Ribandar, into Merces, St Cruz and Taleigao. The list: 1 mangrove in Taleigao, 3 khazans in St Cruz, 2 mangroves and 2 khazans in Merces. 8 tasks for 10 builds, and homes at risk 50.

**2031 to 2039.** I worked through the list on the glowing tiles. Homes at risk dropped 50 → 45 → 43 → 43 → 38 → 35 → 31 → 20 → 16. An egret flew over my new mangroves, which made me smile. Twice the first click on the map after pressing Maya's "Got it" did nothing (see bugs). The game zoomed out on its own in 2039 and showed red-striped homes in several places: south of Altinho, Fontainhas and Patto, north Merces, south Taleigao.

**2040, the last build before the flood.** Maya: "Last call! 14 homes are still in danger, most of them around Taleigao." I tried a house tile (can't build), a water tile ("Small dam: This doesn't change the next storm"), and found a wetland tile next to the striped homes south of Altinho, where I built a khazan.

**Flood 2040.** The camera flew to the Mandovi by Fontainhas and Patto. Dark water spread over the striped homes in Fontainhas, on the Patto island by the Goa State Museum, and in north Merces. 9 lost. "Most of your city held", 2 stars: "In this game 9 homes were lost; three stars allow 8 at most." One home short of 3 stars stung. I saw "Try this storm again" and was tempted, but carried on. Khazans saved 31 and mangroves 12.

**"Coming up: Cyclone and flood in 2050."** Both storms together, with "Where they meet" hatching on the map. The old city lit up with labels: Our Lady of the Immaculate Conception, Adil Shah Palace, Panjim Municipal Market, Institute Menezes Braganza, Jama Masjid, Don Bosco, Kala Academy, Campal Garden. Ruined houses from 2040 still sat in Patto and Fontainhas, which gave the city real memory.

**2041 to 2049.** The list: "Plant 1 mangrove near Atal Setu" and "Build 6 khazans at 4 places, shown on the map". This is where it went wrong for me. I planted a mangrove on a glowing tile near São Tomé and Atal Setu, and neither item ticked. I put a khazan on a glowing tile beside the Goa State Museum: no tick, and homes at risk stayed at 26. Then I clicked the khazan line in the card, the camera moved, and it showed other glowing tiles; those did tick (1/6 … 5/6). One more khazan on a glowing tile in north Merces didn't tick either, though it cut homes at risk 19 → 14. I never found a "near Atal Setu" tile that counted (the tile at the Atal Setu dot only offers a Small dam). In 2049 Maya switched to "Campal is most at risk". I planted pandanus on the Campal beach; homes at risk stayed at 13.

**2050, the last build.** In front of Campal the sea tile offered Seawall or Breakwater. Caring about the Campal homes, I picked the Seawall, "the strongest wall against cyclone surge".

**Cyclone and flood 2050.** The water came down the river channel behind Campal, not from the sea where my seawall stood. The whole Campal waterfront went under, and later Panjim Market, Institute Menezes Braganza and both Don Bosco schools were "Damaged". 12 lost, 2 stars (42 of 54). The card said "The wind alone took one of the homes lost. In this game pandanus shields the homes within 2 tiles of it." Seawall saved 1.

**Final card.** "Panjim, 2026 to 2050": 99 of 120 homes saved, +52 biodiversity, 7 of 9 stars, **Panjim cleared**. The game rule for stars is spelled out at last (30% / 55% / 85% plus biodiversity). I'd have liked to know those thresholds before the 2040 flood.

## 3. What confused me

1. **Glowing tiles that didn't count.** In 2041 to 2048, three builds on glowing tiles didn't advance "6 khazans at 4 places" or "1 mangrove near Atal Setu". I couldn't tell which glow belonged to which task. *What would have helped:* glow only the tiles that count for the open tasks (or colour-code them per task), and say "this counts for: 6 khazans" in the build menu, the way it already says "This doesn't change the next storm".
2. **"Near Atal Setu".** I never found the spot. The Atal Setu dot is on open river where only a Small dam can go. *Helped:* make clicking that task pan to the tile, the way the khazan task did.
3. **Clicking a task moves the camera.** I found this by accident in 2043. *Helped:* a hint the first time ("Tap a task to see where").
4. **Maya pointed the wrong way in 2040.** "Most of them around Taleigao", but the homes I lost were in Fontainhas, Patto and north Merces. *Helped:* name the place where the most homes will actually be lost, or show a number on each red cluster.
5. **Why did my 2049 pandanus and 2050 seawall not lower homes at risk?** Neither showed "doesn't change the next storm", so I thought they would help. Then Campal drowned from the river side. *Helped:* show the expected change in homes at risk on each build card before I commit ("−3 homes at risk").
6. **The earlier Merces khazan didn't count** toward the later "Build 2 khazans in Merces" task. *Helped:* count existing builds, or say "new" in the task.
7. **Star thresholds** were only fully explained on the final card. The 2040 card ("three stars allow 8 at most") came after the fact. *Helped:* show the 3-star target homes number in the "Coming up" card.
8. **"Miramar Beach: Damaged"** after a 9/9 cyclone. Damaged how? It never mattered again. *Helped:* a line of what damage means.

## 4. Where I was bored

- **2026 to 2030**: the cyclone was solved after 3 builds (homes at risk 0), so builds 4 and 5 were just filler and the storm was a formality.
- **2031 to 2039**: ticking a list of 8 glowing tiles, one per year, with Maya facts in between. Pleasant, but it's a chore list rather than a decision. I would have liked something to happen in the city in those years (new houses, a festival, a news line).
- The storms themselves are short, which is good, but the replay after 2040 and 2050 repeats it defence by defence, and I just waited.

## 5. Where I smiled

- Zooming into Fontainhas and Altinho and seeing little people walking, boats, the Mahalaxmi Temple and Kala Academy named on hover.
- The egret flying over my new mangroves in 2033.
- The swirl of the cyclone on the sea in 2030.
- Maya's facts with sources ("Studies found 100 m of mangroves cut wave height by 13% to 66%", "As reported by Lobo (2020): when a khazan bund breaks, mangroves grow back over the old fields").
- "A khazan and mangroves side by side protect each other." It made me want to place them together.
- The tutorial's flood card explaining cyclone defences saved 0 because it was a river flood. That is clear teaching.
- Ruined houses staying ruined after 2040. Sad, but it made the city feel real.

## 7. Fun signals

- **First build:** Tutorial at about 65 s (4 actions). Panjim at about 90 s into Panjim (about 13 actions, mostly zooming and hovering by choice); about 6 minutes from the menu if you count the Tutorial.
- **Whole run:** Tutorial about 4.5 min, Panjim about 12 min, about 17 min in all.
- **My real choices** (at least two good options, different outcomes): about **5**.
  1. 2040 last build: follow Maya's "Taleigao" or defend the striped Fontainhas/Patto/north Merces homes. I picked the south Altinho cluster and lost the others.
  2. After the 2040 flood (one home short of 3 stars): "Try this storm again" or carry on.
  3. 2049 Campal beach: Dune, Pandanus or Seawall.
  4. 2050 last build: Seawall or Breakwater in front of Campal, or the 6th khazan for the objective.
  5. Mangrove or Khazan on each wetland tile (the game hints they work best side by side).
  Most of the other 20 builds were "click the glowing tile". The 2030 builds after the dune and pandanus weren't choices at all (0 homes at risk).
- **Did one pattern always win?** Yes: khazans. They saved 31 homes in each flood and dominated every breakdown. Following the glowing tiles plus khazans everywhere on the wetlands looks like the winning recipe. Beach defences barely mattered after 2030.
- **Storm results that surprised me:**
  - 2030 cyclone: trivially 9/9 after only 3 builds.
  - 2040 flood: losses were in Fontainhas, Patto and north Merces, not "around Taleigao" as Maya said.

  - *Cyclone 2030 (0 lost):* nothing lost.

## 9. Would I play again? Would I tell a friend?

**Play again:** yes, once. I want to fix the 2040 flood (I was one home off 3 stars) and see whether a mangrove and khazan line along the Campal river side saves the waterfront in 2050. After that, probably not: the middle years are a checklist, and I suspect "khazans on every glowing wetland" always wins.

**Tell a friend:** yes. I'd say: "It's a gentle little game about Panjim and its storms. The map is the real city, you can zoom in on Fontainhas and the churches and temples, and you learn why khazans and mangroves matter. It takes 15 minutes. Don't trust every glowing tile, and watch where the river goes, not just the sea."

## 10. Verdict

A charming, real-feeling Panjim and good teaching, let down in the late game by glowing tiles that don't count and hints that point the wrong way.

### The story and the city

Panjim felt alive in a way most strategy maps don't. The streets are real, the neighbourhoods have names, and hovering finds the Mahalaxmi Temple, Kala Academy or Adil Shah Palace. Little people walk about, boats float, an egret comes to the mangroves, and ruined houses stay ruined after a flood. Those ruins in Patto beside the Goa State Museum were the moment I actually cared. Maya is likeable, and her sourced facts taught me things (khazans, bunds, mangrove wave studies). But there isn't really a story: no residents with names, and nothing happens between storms except her facts. Over 25 years the city didn't visibly change or grow apart from my builds and the damage. I cared about "the homes" as red stripes and a counter, not as people. One named family per neighbourhood, or a line of news each year, would turn a good-looking map into a place I'd mourn.

## Knowledge questions (asked after play)

1. **What does a mangrove do in a storm?** The game said mangrove forests slow the flow of the water, lower a storm's surge and cut the height of its waves. In this game they help in both kinds of storm (cyclone and river flood). Maya added that mangroves on a khazan's outer bund work as wave breakers.
2. **Why can a seawall be risky?** It costs 5 biodiversity, the most of any defence. It pushes water to its ends, and it breaks in its 2nd cyclone or once it is 10 years old ("cracks with age").
3. **What is a khazan, and what is it for?** Goa's old farmed coastal wetland, reclaimed from marsh: a bund and a sluice gate round paddy fields and fish ponds, shared by farmers and fishers. It holds back river floods by keeping the floodwater in its fields, so nearby homes stay drier; it is little help in a cyclone.
4. **Where did the 2040 flood come from, and why from there rather than the sea?** Maya said it comes down the Mandovi, through the channel at Ribandar, into Merces, St Cruz and Taleigao. The game called it a monsoon (river) flood, but it never explained why it comes from there rather than the sea, so on the "why": I don't know.
5. **What helps homes against a cyclone's wind?** Pandanus. The game said it is the only defence that shields homes from a cyclone's wind, and that it shields the homes within 2 tiles of it.
