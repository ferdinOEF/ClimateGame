# Riptide Rising: festival visitor diary (round 1)

## 1. Who I am and what I wanted

I'm a festival visitor in Panjim. I walked up to a big screen and had about 10 minutes. I'd never seen the game. I skim, skip long paragraphs, and click whatever glows or looks inviting. I never opened "?" or "Facts & sources". I wanted to save some houses, feel clever, and leave knowing a little more about my own city.

Visitor-time tally: about 2 to 4 s per click and 4 words/s of reading, with storm-watching counted at real time. My own thinking time is not counted.

## 2. The play, in order

**Main menu (0 to 9 s).** A good-looking 3D hex map of Goa, "Riptide Rising", "Three storms. One build a year." The orange Tutorial button is the loudest thing on the screen, so I pressed it.

**Tutorial (9 to 140 s).**
- The intro card has one short paragraph and a red risk map. I pressed Begin. Maya appeared with "The glowing card and tiles show what to try", which told me exactly what to do.
- 2026: I clicked the glowing beach tile and a menu of three cards came up. I picked the glowing Dune. **This was my first build, at about 34 visitor seconds and 4 actions.** Homes at risk dropped from 22 to 18, which felt satisfying straight away.
- 2027 to 2029: pandanus, then a breakwater in the glowing water, then a seawall. Each time the "homes at risk" number fell (18, 12, 2, 0). It's easy and it rewards you. I didn't need to read anything.
- Cyclone 2029: it took about 7 s. "Your city came through", 3 stars, with a bar per defence. Clear.
- 2030 to 2032: mangrove, khazan and small dam on the glowing estuary and river tiles. The flood took about 22 s. 3 stars, 26 of 26 saved, and a summary of all 7 defences. Then I pressed "Play Panjim".
- How it felt: very guided. It felt a bit like filling in a colouring book, but it was quick and I understood the two storm types.

**Play Panjim, first stretch (140 to 250 s).**
- I skipped the intro paragraph (it's long) and read only the bold line, "Save as many homes as you can from three storms".
- 2026 to 2028: the checklist said 1 dune and 2 pandanus in front of Sharada Mandir School. All three tiles glowed and I built them. Homes at risk went from 9 to 0.
- 2029: the next item was "Build 1 khazan in Merces". I didn't know where Merces was, so I dragged the map around until I found the glowing tile beside the Merces label. Built it.
- 2030, the last build before the cyclone: nothing glowed and homes at risk was already 0. This was a real choice, so I added a breakwater out at sea in front of the school "to be safe".
- **Cyclone 2030:** a short storm, then a replay ("With no defences at all: 9 homes hit"). 3 stars, 9 of 9 saved, "Objective complete". Miramar Beach was marked "Damaged". The breakdown showed Dune 5, Pandanus 4, **Breakwater 0**. My one free choice had done nothing, and it cost biodiversity. I felt a bit silly, but at least the game told me.

**Monsoon flood stretch (250 to 430 s).**
- "Monsoon flood in 2040, 10 years away", with a red map. Ten builds felt like a lot for a festival.
- The checklist had four items: 1 mangrove in Taleigao, 3 khazans in St Cruz, 2 mangroves in Merces, 2 khazans in Merces. Six or seven tiles glowed.
- 2031 to 2036: I built mangroves and khazans on the glowing tiles. Merces mangroves went to 2/2, Merces khazans to 1/2 and St Cruz khazans to 2/3. Homes at risk went 50, 45, 43, 38, 35, 31.
- 2037 to 2038: I built a mangrove and then a khazan on the last two glowing tiles near Taleigao. **Neither one ticked anything on the checklist** (Taleigao mangrove stayed 0/1, St Cruz khazans stayed 2/3). Homes at risk still fell, to 21 and then 16. I didn't know what I'd done wrong.
- 2039 to 2040: nothing glowed any more. I clicked a land tile near Taleigao and nothing happened at all. I tried a wetland tile and built a mangrove, but homes at risk stayed at 16, so that build was wasted. Maya said "Taleigao is most at risk", and the camera swung to show red-striped homes near Altinho. I put two khazans on wetland tiles next to them and got down to 15.
- **Flood 2040:** about 45 s of storm. It was long, but it looked lovely: rain, the camera following the water, and an "Absorbed" tag popping up over a khazan. Result: "Most of your city held", 2 stars, 46 of 57 saved, 11 lost. The breakdown showed Khazan 29 and Mangrove 12.

**Final stretch (430 to 585 s).**
- "Cyclone and flood in 2050, 10 years away". The labels on the map got very crowded (about 12 landmark names on screen at once).
- The checklist said 2 mangroves at Taleigao and near Goa State Museum, and 5 khazans "shown on the map". I built khazans on the glowing tiles and they ticked to 5/5. My mangrove on a glowing tile near Atal Setu did **not** tick.
- Later, a mangrove I placed on an unlit wetland tile next to a "Taleigao" tag (now east of the river?) did tick: 1/2.
- The cyclone arrow pointed at Campal, so I built a seawall on the beach. Homes at risk didn't change. Then I built a seawall in the sea in front of Campal (14 down to 12) and a pandanus on the beach for my last build. Biodiversity was still high, so the seawalls felt affordable. This was a real choice.
- **2050 storm:** I watched for about 4 s and already had 10 homes lost, so I pressed Speed up. No replay appeared. Result: "Most of your city held", 2 stars, 42 of 52 saved, and 7 landmarks "Damaged" (market, Menezes Braganza, Don Bosco, and so on). Breakdown: Khazan 30, Mangrove 12, Pandanus 5, Seawall 4.
- **Final card:** "Panjim, 2026 to 2050": 97 of 118 homes saved, +41 biodiversity, **7 of 9 stars, Panjim cleared**. Play again, or Back to the menu.

## 3. What confused me

1. **Glowing tiles and the checklist disagree.** Some builds on glowing tiles never ticked their item. The second Merces khazan and the Taleigao mangrove never got a glowing tile at all. *What would help:* each glowing tile should say which item it is for, for example a little "Taleigao: mangrove" tag, and every item should have a glowing tile.
2. **I didn't know where neighbourhoods were.** "Build 1 khazan in Merces" meant nothing to me until I panned around. The "Taleigao" tag seemed to appear in two places (south-west early on, then east of the river beside the Mandovi waterfront). *What would help:* clicking a checklist line should fly the camera to that place.
3. **The build menu often opens far from the tile I clicked,** for example at the top of the screen when I clicked near the bottom, and I couldn't see which tile was selected. *What would help:* a clear highlight on the chosen tile, with the menu anchored next to it.
4. **"Homes at risk" doesn't match the result card.** The HUD said 15 at risk before the 2040 flood, but the result said 57 homes in the path and 11 lost. *What would help:* the same word and number before and after, for example "15 homes will be hit / 42 more will get wet".
5. **Clicking an ordinary land tile does nothing.** There's no "can't build here" message. *What would help:* a tiny toast such as "Homes here: build on the beach or wetland next to them".
6. **Which builds actually count?** My breakwater out at sea scored 0, the beach seawall didn't move "homes at risk", and one mangrove did nothing. Nothing showed me before I built that it would be useless. *What would help:* a preview of "-N homes at risk" on each menu card before I confirm.
7. **Damaged and Stood tags.** "Every home in the path survived", yet Miramar Beach was "Damaged". Is the beach a home? *What would help:* separate the landmark line from the homes line, or say "beach eroded".
8. **Text mixes up storm words.** During flood prep, Maya said "Mangroves slow the surge". In the tutorial, the mangrove card said "waves and surge" while the side panel also said "River flood".

## 4. Where I was bored

- **The 10-builds-before-each-storm stretches (2031 to 2040 and 2041 to 2050).** That's 20 builds with no storm. After the guided tiles run out there's nothing to aim at, so I clicked about hoping.
- **The 2040 flood took about 45 s of real time** plus a replay. That's long at a festival, and I didn't find the Speed up button until the last storm.
- **The run was too long for my slot:** tutorial about 2.3 min plus Panjim about 7.4 min comes to roughly 10 min, which used up all my time.
- The intro paragraph of Panjim and the long "game rule" text on the final card. I skipped both.

## 5. Where I smiled

- The 3D city of Panjim with real place names (Miramar, Campal, Fontainhas, Altinho, Kala Academy). Recognising my own city was the best part.
- Birds (a gull and a kingfisher) flying over the map after I planted mangroves.

## 10. Verdict

A beautiful, readable first five minutes in a city I recognise. After that the long build stretches, the checklist that doesn't tick, and the unexplained losses wear it down, and the whole run doesn't fit a festival slot.

## Knowledge questions (asked after play)

1. **What does a mangrove do in a storm?** It slows the flow of the water, lowering the surge and cutting the height of the waves. It helps in both a cyclone and a river flood, and Maya said 100 m of mangroves cut wave height by 13% to 66%.
2. **Why can a seawall be risky?** It costs the most biodiversity (−5, and Maya said nothing lives on it), and it pushes water round its ends. It also cracks with age and breaks in its second cyclone or once it is 10 years old.
3. **What is a khazan, and what is it for?** It's Goa's old farmed wetland: a bund and a sluice gate round paddy fields and fish ponds, shared by farmers and fishers. It holds floodwater in its fields so nearby homes stay drier, which makes it the river-flood defence, though it's little help in a cyclone.
4. **Where did the 2040 flood come from, and why from there rather than the sea?** Maya said it came down the Mandovi, through the channel at Ribandar, into Merces, St Cruz and Taleigao. As for why from the river rather than the sea, I don't know; the game only called it a monsoon river flood and never explained it.
5. **What helps homes against a cyclone's wind?** Pandanus (screw pine). The game called it the only defence that shields homes from a cyclone's wind, because a row of it shelters what is behind it.
