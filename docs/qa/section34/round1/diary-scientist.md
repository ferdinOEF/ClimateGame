# Riptide Rising: playtest diary, the coastal scientist (Section 34, round 1)

## 1. Who I am and what I wanted

I'm a coastal geomorphologist. I work on nature-based defences: mangroves, dunes, beach vegetation such as pandanus, and on how they compare with seawalls and breakwaters against storm surge and river floods. I know Goa's khazans. I came in with two goals. First, to play one honest run of "Play Panjim" to its final card, plus the Tutorial. Second, to check every claim the game makes about the real world against the source it names. Anything that is only a rule of this game should say so, and should not quietly teach something false about real coasts.

I judged only what the screen showed me. I opened the cited sources on the web where I could. I used the driver's `/waitcalm` to wait out storms.

Time tally (a real player's time: about 3 s per click and 4 words a second of reading; my own thinking and web checks are not counted):
- Main menu, then the whole Facts & sources screen, read before playing: about 5.5 min (around 1,300 words).
- Tutorial: about 5 min. **First build ever: about 60 s after clicking Tutorial, 4 actions** (Tutorial, Begin, a tile, the Dune card). Counting the Facts reading first, that was about 6.5 min into the session.
- Play Panjim: about 16 min, start to final card. **First Panjim build: about 4 min in, 9 actions**, because I read Help and the Field Guide first. Without those it would have been about 45 s.
- Whole session: about 26 min.

## 4. Game rules that might teach something false about the real world

They are all honestly labelled "in this game". But a player, and especially a student, will carry the *pattern* home, not the label.

1. **Nature-based defences work at full strength the year they're planted.** The Field Guide says: "pandanus is at full strength from the year it is planted"; "a mangrove has one strength from the year it is planted". In my run one freshly planted mangrove tile took 11 homes out of a flood's way. Real mangrove plantings take years (often 5 to 10 or more) to build the stem density that attenuates water, and many plantings fail. Dunes need vegetation to establish. The lesson the game teaches is "plant now, protected next year". That is the single most common misconception in nature-based-solutions policy. Fix: a short growth ramp (half strength for the first few years), or at least a caption: "In real life mangroves take years to grow before they protect."

2. **"A khazan ... is never worn, so it never needs a repair."** This is the opposite of the central real-world lesson about khazans. They survive only through constant community maintenance of bunds and sluice gates, and the game's own cited source (Lobo 2020) says the breaches come from "lack of ... maintenance". Fix: make khazans need upkeep, or drop the "never needs a repair" sentence and say "In this game khazans don't wear out; real ones need their bunds kept up."

3. **Mangroves are the strongest defence against a monsoon river flood.** Tutorial flood: Mangrove 11 against Small dam 8. Panjim 2040: Mangrove 24, Khazan 18, Small Dam 6. The cited evidence (McIvor Report 2) is about *coastal* surge and waves, at 5 to 50 cm of surge reduction per *kilometre* of forest. A game tile is 135 m, so one patch would lower a surge by only a few centimetres. Dense mangroves in a channel can even raise water levels upstream through friction. Fix: weaken mangroves against river floods and keep them strong against surge, or say outright that the river-flood effect is a game simplification.

4. **Small dams "hold back river floods" and are "the strongest stop for a river flood".** Bandharas are irrigation and storage weirs. The game's own cited article says they are opened during floods, and that a breached mud bandhara *causes* flash floods. Small check dams store almost nothing compared with a monsoon flood volume. Fix: recast the small dam as a weak, risky flood defence, or relabel its flood role clearly ("In this game only; real bandharas store water and are opened in floods").

5. **Seawalls can be built on open-sea tiles.** I built one offshore at Campal. Offshore, it behaves as a detached breakwater. This blurs the very distinction the Tutorial teaches. The 10-year or second-storm failure rule is also much shorter than a real design life, though it is clearly labelled and works as a stand-in for "maintenance and overtopping risk".

6. **"Pandanus is the only defence that lowers the wind."** Clearly a game rule, but mangrove belts and coastal shelterbelts also cut wind. A line such as "In this game only pandanus..." would be more precise than "the only defence".

7. **Breakwaters cost biodiversity.** It's debatable: rubble-mound breakwaters are often colonised by reef organisms. Minor, and labelled.

## 5. What confused me

- **"Homes at risk" didn't move after the first guided dune** (9 → 9), even though Maya's first objective sent me there. I couldn't tell whether the dune was useless or the counter lagged.
- **Pressing R (Show risk) hid the red heat** and showed a big outline instead. Pressing it again brought the red back. Help says R shows "the homes the next storm would hit today", so I expected it to add information, not take it away.
- **Glowing tiles don't say what to build**, and the objective areas don't match the map labels. A khazan I put on the tile beside the "Atal Setu" label counted toward "Ourem creek", and the next tile over counted as Atal Setu. A mangrove on a glowing tile next to "Taleigao" didn't tick "Plant 1 mangrove at Taleigao".
- **The objective glow put me on a tile whose menu said "This doesn't change the next storm"** (2049, east of Taleigao). Why would Maya ask for a build that does nothing?
- **The "This doesn't change the next storm" note against what happened**: every sand tile at Campal carried it, yet the wind then took 5 Campal homes, and pandanus is the only wind defence (bug 1).
- **"Lost in earlier storms: 10"** on the 2050 card. I'd lost 1 + 14 = 15 homes in earlier storms. I think it means "10 of this storm's path homes were already gone", but it doesn't say so.
- **Three definitions of the "what protected your homes" numbers** (section 3b).
- **The biodiversity counter lagged**: it stayed at +32 after a mangrove (+3) and at +41 after a breakwater (−2), and only caught up on the result card.
- **The star rule's "biodiversity up 6"** for three stars in a storm wasn't obvious until I read the Help small print. The final card explained it well.
- A tile in the middle of the Merces fields said "Outside Panjim: this creek corner is not part of this game".
- The 2050 "Coming up" legend lists Adil Shah Palace, but the mini-map doesn't label it.

## 6. Where I was bored

- 2031 to 2038: a long run of "click the glowing tile, pick Khazan or Mangrove". The objective list made the flood decade feel like a checklist, not a puzzle.
- The flood animation in 2040 ran about 40 s plus a replay. Speed up is there, but I'd have liked to skip straight to the result after the first viewing.
- The Facts screen is very long (about 1,300 words). As a scientist I read it all with pleasure; a casual player won't.

## 7. Where I smiled

- The Facts & sources screen exists at all. Every entry has a full citation and a note on exactly what the source says. "As reported by Lobo (2020)" for a blog, and "The research is about dunes in England and Wales", are careful, honest scoping that I rarely see in educational games.
- Help ends with "Everything on this page is a rule of this game." Lovely.
- Tutorial flood cards: "A cyclone defence; this was a river flood". Clear, kind and accurate.
- The 2030 result card: "The wind alone took one of the homes lost. In this game pandanus shields the homes within 2 tiles of it." That is exactly the right way to explain a loss and label the rule in one breath.
- "Cyclone and flood in 2050 ... Where they meet" teaches compound flooding, which is real and under-taught.
- Seeing an egret and a kingfisher fly over my khazans and mangroves. (I never managed to tap one.)
- The real Panjim landmarks (Kala Academy, Campal Garden, Mahalaxmi Temple) and the hand-drawn creek network on real OSM and Copernicus data.

## 11. Would I play again? Would I tell a colleague?

**Play again: yes.** I want to try the 2050 storm with pandanus at Campal and keep biodiversity at +40 or above, and to see whether a seawall-free run can still get three stars. The biodiversity-versus-homes trade-off at the end was a genuinely good decision.

**Tell a colleague: yes, with a caveat.** I would say: "It's a well-made Goa coast game whose Facts page is more honest than most textbooks. Every real-world claim is cited and scoped, and the game rules are labelled. But don't let students walk away thinking a mangrove planted this year stops a river flood next year, that khazans never need upkeep, or that a bandhara is a flood defence. Use it to start that conversation."

## 12. Verdict in one line

Unusually honest about its sources and good at labelling its rules, but a few of those labelled rules (instant-strength mangroves, maintenance-free khazans, small dams as flood defences) still teach the wrong lesson, and the "doesn't change the next storm" hint ignores wind.

## Knowledge questions (asked after play)

Answered only from what the game showed me.

1. **What does a mangrove do in a storm?** The game says mangroves slow the flow of the water, lower a storm's surge and cut the height of its waves (100 m of mangroves cut wave height by 13% to 66%). In this game they help in both cyclones and river floods, and three or more side by side protect each other.
2. **Why can a seawall be risky?** In this game a seawall cracks with age and breaks in its second cyclone, or in its next one once it is 10 years old, and it pushes extra water onto the land at each end. It also costs 5 biodiversity, and Maya said that if a storm is bigger than the wall, it fails all at once.
3. **What is a khazan, and what is it for?** A khazan is one of Goa's coastal wetlands, reclaimed from marsh: a bund and a sluice gate round paddy fields and fish ponds, with bunds that keep out the tide and land that farmers and fishers share. In this game it holds back river floods, so nearby homes stay drier.
4. **Where did the 2040 flood come from, and why from there rather than the sea?** Maya said (tagged Game rule) that it comes down the Mandovi, through the channel at Ribandar, into Merces, St Cruz and Taleigao. The game called it a monsoon river flood but never said why it comes from there rather than the sea, so on the "why" I don't know.
5. **What helps homes against a cyclone's wind?** Pandanus: the game says it is the only defence that lowers the wind, and homes within 2 tiles of it take half the wind damage. Its caption says a row of it shelters what is behind it from the wind.
