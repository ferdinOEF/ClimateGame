# Playtest diary: the Panjim local

## 1. Who I am and what I wanted

I've lived in Panjim all my life. I walk the Campal and Miramar stretch, I know where water stands in Fontainhas and São Tomé every monsoon, and I've seen the St Inez nallah overflow. I picked "Play Panjim", not the Tutorial, because I wanted to see my own city. I had two goals: play one full run to the last card, and check whether the board feels like Panjim to someone who lives here. I checked every label, where the water goes, and what counts as high ground.

## 2. The play, in order

Player-time tally: about 3 s per click, plus reading at 4 words a second.

- **Menu to the board (0 to about 45 s).** I read the menu and clicked Play Panjim. The intro card says 25 years, one build a year, three storms, and shows a mini-map of the cyclone heading for Sharada Mandir School. I clicked Begin. Maya pointed me to the checklist at bottom right: 1 dune and 2 pandanus in front of Sharada Mandir. Two beach hexes glowed. The opening felt quick and clear.
- **2026: first build at about 69 s (4 actions).** I clicked the glowing beach hex in front of the school and picked Dune. Homes at risk went from 9 to 4. It was satisfying straight away.
- **2027 and 2028: two pandanus on the beach.** Homes at risk went 4, then 3, then 0. The checklist ticked off, then switched to an optional "Get ahead: the monsoon flood: build 1 khazan in Merces".
- **2029: khazan in Merces.** I zoomed out and found Merces in the east. A khazan in Merces is exactly right for a local. Before this I'd spent a while hovering landmarks: Institute Menezes Braganza, Adil Shah Palace, Campal Garden, Kala Academy, Don Bosco, Mahalaxmi Temple, Goa State Museum at Patto, Government Polytechnic on Altinho.
- **2030: one build before the cyclone.** Homes at risk were already 0. I clicked the St Inez creek water and nothing happened. I put a pandanus on the beach near Campal instead. That build started the cyclone at once. The storm was a ●○○ (weak) one, with rain, swirls, and the beach hexes going under. The result: **★★★, 9 of 9 homes saved.** The dune saved 5 and the pandanus 4. Miramar Beach was marked Damaged, and Sharada Mandir School and Dhempe College Stood.
- **2031 to 2040: the monsoon flood.** Maya says "in this game" the flood comes down the Mandovi, through the channel at Ribandar, and into Merces, St Cruz and Taleigao. There were 50 homes at risk and the checklist asked for 1 mangrove at Taleigao, 3 khazans at St Cruz, 2 mangroves and 2 khazans at Merces. I worked through the glowing hexes. Homes at risk fell 50, 45, 42, 38, 35, 31, 20, 16. Merces needed a second khazan and nothing glowed for it. I placed one on a wetland hex near Merces, but it didn't count (still 1 of 2) and saved only 1 home. Maya then said Taleigao was most at risk, so I put a khazan on the St Inez side by Taleigao: 15 to 10. "Last call: around the city centre." For my last build I chose a **small dam in the Ourem creek** between Fontainhas and Patto. It was the only option offered there. The flood was dramatic: the whole Mandovi went dark navy. The result: **★★★, 51 of 57 saved, 6 lost.** Khazans saved 33, mangroves 9, the dam 4.
- **2041 to 2050: cyclone and flood together.** The cyclone comes in at the Mandovi mouth at Campal and the flood comes down from Ribandar, and they meet at the city centre. That is plausible and a bit frightening for anyone who has seen D.B. Marg under water. The checklist asked for 2 mangroves (Taleigao and the Ourem creek) and 7 khazans at 6 places. I built 2 mangroves and 6 khazans on the glowing hexes, and homes at risk went 33 down to 18. Maya then said Campal was most at risk. I put a **seawall** on the beach in front of Campal. Homes at risk stayed at 18, with no change at all. For my last build I clicked a sea hex to look for the Breakwater, clicked the same spot again, and the menu (which had opened under my cursor) took that second click as **Seawall**. My final build was gone, and the storm started before I could react. The result: **★★☆, 46 of 57 saved, 11 lost.** "The wind alone took 5 of the homes lost. In this game pandanus shields the homes within 2 tiles of it." Institute Menezes Braganza, Don Bosco High School, Kala Academy and Campal Garden were Damaged.
- **Final card:** "Panjim, 2026 to 2050": 106 of 123 homes saved, +41 biodiversity, **8 of 9 stars, Panjim cleared.**

## 3. Places, names and geography: what felt right, what felt wrong

**What felt right (and it's a lot):**

- **The overall shape** (local-board-start.jpg, local-campal-zoom.jpg). The sea is to the west. Miramar beach runs north to the Mandovi mouth at Campal, with Caranzalem below Miramar and Dona Paula at the south-west tip. Taleigao is inland to the south. The north bank (Betim, Reis Magos) is drawn as raised grey ground. It reads as Panjim at a glance.
- **Altinho as a raised hill**, with the Government Polytechnic on it. That's correct and nicely done.
- **Fontainhas on the creek, Patto across the Ourem creek, the Goa State Museum labelled "Museum, Patto".** All correct. São Tomé sits just north of Fontainhas towards the river, which is also correct.
- **Khazans in Merces and St Cruz, mangroves on the khazan's outer bund, the Lobo (2020) note** that broken bunds turn back into mangrove. This is real Goan knowledge, and it made me smile.
- **What floods.** Fontainhas and Patto flood (local-ourem-dam.jpg, red-hatched hexes by the creek), and so do Campal and the riverfront in the combined storm. That matches what every resident knows. The final storm hitting the Campal / Kala Academy / Campal Garden strip felt very true.
- **Sharada Mandir School and Dhempe College at Miramar, Mahalaxmi Temple near Fontainhas, Campal Garden at Campal.** All in the right places.
- **Pandanus**: "its leaves are woven into mats". That's right, and a nice local touch.

**What jarred:**

- **"The Mandovi waterfront" label is in the wrong place** (local-waterfront-label.jpg, label at about x=1290, y=510). It sits out between Patto and Merces, over the khazan channel. For any local, the Mandovi waterfront is D.B. Marg along the north edge of the city, from Campal past the Old Secretariat to the ferry wharf. This is the biggest name jar in the game.
- **The St Inez creek is drawn as a wide lake** running from Campal down to Taleigao (local-campal-zoom.jpg, the blue band west of Altinho). The real St Inez nallah is a narrow, often choked channel. Likewise, the Ourem creek and the channel between Patto/Fontainhas and Merces read as a broad river, almost as wide as the Mandovi itself (local-taleigao-risk.jpg). I understand hexes are coarse, but it makes the city look like an island chain.
- **The flood path is drawn as a straight dashed line over dry land**, from Ribandar diagonally through St Cruz to Taleigao (local-taleigao-risk.jpg, the dashed line crossing the green hexes). Real floods here follow the creeks and khazan fields. A line cutting across Merces and St Cruz land reads wrong.
- **A small dam in the Ourem creek** (local-ourem-dam.jpg). The tooltip says "Stores water for the dry season", but the Ourem creek is tidal and brackish. Nobody stores dry-season water there. A sluice gate would ring truer to a Goan than a "small dam".
- **Atal Setu is only a floating label** over the water (local-campal-zoom.jpg, top right). The bridge itself, the biggest thing on Panjim's skyline today, isn't drawn.
- **Landmark order along the riverfront, as I remember it** (local-waterfront-label.jpg): the Institute Menezes Braganza label sits west of Adil Shah Palace, and the Panjim Municipal Market is on the riverfront row. To me, IMB sits a little east of the Old Secretariat by Azad Maidan, and the Municipal Market is inland, south of the centre, not on the river. These are small offsets, but a local notices them.
- **Kala Academy** is drawn inland on the creek side of Campal. The real one fronts the Mandovi on D.B. Marg.
- **Names are inconsistent.** The map says "Santa Inez" while the quests say "St Cruz". Locals say "St Inez" (or "Santa Inez") and "St Cruz" / "Santa Cruz". Pick one style. **Ribandar** is named in Maya's flood rule but labelled nowhere on the map, so I couldn't find "the channel at Ribandar". The **Caranzalem** label is so faint I nearly missed it.
- **The Government Polytechnic label jumps around** between views (local-campal-zoom.jpg, then local-waterfront-label.jpg, then later to the west side of Altinho). It made me doubt the label placement in general.

## 4. What confused me

1. **Which neighbourhood a hex belongs to.** The build menu never says "Merces" or "St Cruz". My khazan near Merces didn't count for "Build 2 khazans in Merces" and nothing told me why. *What would help:* show the area's name in the build menu header ("Khazan, Merces").
2. **Where each defence can go.** Every menu says "Not here: Breakwater…". I only found the Breakwater by accident on a sea hex in 2050. *What would help:* when I hover a defence, light up the hexes where it's allowed.
3. **The seawall didn't move "Homes at risk" (18 stayed 18)**, yet afterwards the game said the seawalls saved 7. *What would help:* a preview that matches the result, or text saying "this will help in the storm, but it isn't counted in the preview".
4. **The wind rule ("pandanus shields homes within 2 tiles")** only appeared on the last result card, after it had cost me 5 homes. *What would help:* tell me before the storm, the way Maya warned me about Taleigao and Campal.
5. **The mangrove card says "in both storms"** when the game has three. *What would help:* "in cyclones and floods".
6. **"46 of 57 homes saved / Lost in earlier storms: 6".** I couldn't tell whether those 6 are inside the 57 or extra. *What would help:* one clear line, such as "this storm: 11 lost".
7. **Clicking some hexes does nothing** (the St Inez water, open sea, some Ourem land), with no message. *What would help:* a short "Nothing can be built here" toast.
8. **The strength dots ●○○ / ●●● versus the stars on the timeline.** I only understood the dots after hovering the timeline and reading "the dots show how strong".

## 5. Where I was bored

- **2031 to 2039**: eight builds of "click the glowing hex, pick what the checklist says". By the fourth khazan it was colouring by numbers. The only thinking was the last build or two of each storm.
- **The monsoon flood animation** ran about 25 s, then the replay panel. I didn't press Speed up, and I wish I had.
- **The replay panels plus "See what protected" plus "Coming up"**: three cards in a row after every storm. The information is good but it's slow.

## 6. Where I smiled

- Seeing "Sharada Mandir School" and "Dhempe College" stand after the first cyclone.
- Khazans in Merces and the Lobo (2020) note about bunds breaking and mangroves coming back.
- Fontainhas and Patto flooding in the monsoon. That's exactly what happens.
- The Goa State Museum labelled "Museum, Patto", and the Government Polytechnic on top of Altinho.
- The whole Mandovi turning dark navy in the 2040 flood.
- "Panjim cleared."

   - **Expected:** the menu closes or stays open, since I hadn't chosen anything.
   - **Got:** Seawall was built, using my last build of the game, and the final storm started at once.
   - **Screenshot:** local-double-seawall.jpg ("Seawalls ×2").
2. **MINOR: the first map click after dismissing Maya ("Got it") is swallowed.**

   - **Got:** nothing, and a second click was needed. This happened 5 or 6 times in one run.

3. **MINOR to MAJOR: the seawall preview doesn't match the result.**
   - **Steps:** in 2049, with 18 homes at risk around Campal, build a Seawall on the Campal beach hex.
   - **Expected:** Homes at risk drops.
   - **Got:** it stayed at 18, yet the result says the seawalls saved 7. The preview misled my last decision.

6. **MINOR: the Government Polytechnic label changes position** between views (on Altinho at about x=820 in one view, west of Altinho at about x=538 in another).

8. **MINOR: the build menu sometimes opens far from the hex** I clicked (for example, clicking a hex at the bottom left opened the menu near the top centre), so my eye had to jump.

## 8. Fun signals

- **First build** at about **69 seconds** of player time and **4 actions** (Play Panjim, Begin, the hex, Dune).
- **The whole run** took about **840 s of player time (about 14 minutes)**, 25 builds.
- **My real choices** (at least two good options with different outcomes): **4**.
  1. 2029: an extra Miramar defence, or "get ahead" with a khazan in Merces.
  2. 2040, last flood build: a small dam in the Ourem creek (to protect Fontainhas and Patto), or the second Merces khazan for the checklist. I chose the dam, and it saved 4.
  3. 2049: Seawall (strongest, −5 biodiversity), Dune, or Pandanus at Campal. I chose the seawall, and with hindsight pandanus would have stopped the wind losses.
  4. 2050, last build: finish the khazan checklist (7 of 7), or add a Breakwater or more beach defence at Campal. The double-click took it out of my hands.
  Everything else was following the glow.
- **Did one pattern always win?** Yes: **khazans on the glowing wetland hexes**. They saved 33 homes in 2040 and 27 in 2050. Following the checklist exactly clears the game. On the beach, a dune plus pandanus in front of the target cleared the first cyclone with no thought needed.
- **Storm results that surprised me:**
  - The seawall showed 0 change in the preview but "saved 7" in the result.
  - The dune is said to survive a beating, yet mid-storm the beach hexes looked fully drowned.
  - 2040 still gave 3 stars with the Merces objective unfinished and 6 homes lost.
  - 5 homes in 2050 went to wind, which no preview had warned me about.
- **Why I lost homes, after each storm's result cards:**
  - **2030 cyclone:** I lost none. The dune and pandanus in front of the school covered the whole path, and the cards said so clearly (dune 5, pandanus 4).
  - **2040 flood:** I lost 6, mostly, I think, in the Merces cluster where my second Merces khazan landed on a hex that didn't count, plus one at Campal. The replay showed the hit hexes, but the result card never named the neighbourhoods, so this was only half clear.
  - **2050 cyclone and flood:** I lost 11 around Campal: 5 to wind, because no pandanus was within 2 hexes, and the rest to surge on the Campal shore that my seawalls didn't fully cover. The card explained the wind clearly, but only after the fact. I had no way to know beforehand.

## 10. Would I play again? Would I tell a friend?

- **Play again:** Yes, once. I'd want to beat the 2050 storm with pandanus at Campal and see whether I can save Campal Garden and Kala Academy. After that, no. The middle stretch is checklist-following, and a second run would be the same glowing hexes.
- **Tell a friend:** Yes, especially Panjim friends and teachers. I'd tell them: "It's our city as a hex board. Khazans in Merces, Fontainhas flooding, the museum at Patto, all of it. Twenty minutes, and you'll learn why the khazans matter. Ignore that the 'Mandovi waterfront' label is in Merces, and don't double-click on the last build."

## 11. Verdict in one line

It feels like Panjim, with khazans, Fontainhas floods and Altinho as high ground, but the middle game is paint-by-numbers, the seawall preview and the double-click build cost me my best choice, and the "Mandovi waterfront" label sits in the wrong place.

## Knowledge questions (asked after play)

1. **What does a mangrove do in a storm?** The build card said it "protects against waves and surge in both storms", and Maya said mangroves "slow the surge before it reaches the houses". She also said "100 m of mangroves cut wave height by 13% to 66%, mostly for waves under 70 cm", and that mangroves on a khazan's outer bund "work as wave breakers against the tide".
2. **Why can a seawall be risky?** The card said it is "the strongest wall against cyclone surge" but "costs nature, and cracks with age". It took 5 off biodiversity when I built it.
3. **What is a khazan, and what is it for?** Maya said khazans are "Goa's coastal wetlands, reclaimed from marsh", where "bunds keep out the tide, and farmers and fishers share the land". In the game a khazan "holds back river floods": it holds floodwater in its fields so that nearby homes stay drier.
4. **Where did the 2040 flood come from, and why from there rather than the sea?** Maya said that "in this game" the flood "comes down the Mandovi, through the channel at Ribandar, into Merces, St Cruz and Taleigao", so it came down the river as a monsoon flood. As for why from there and not the sea: I don't know. The game never explained it.
5. **What helps homes against a cyclone's wind?** Pandanus. Its card said it "shields homes from cyclone waves and wind", and the 2050 result card said "pandanus shields the homes within 2 tiles of it", after the wind alone took 5 of my homes.
